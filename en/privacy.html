Privacy

Last updated: 26 September 2026

In short
  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics and no account.
  • We have no server of our own: data from the apps does not reach us and we don’t store it.
  • To work, Fietsert does send your location or search text to map, search, routing and weather services run by others. They are listed below, with what they receive. They also receive your IP address.
  • Your routes are stored on your device and, on iPhone if you choose, in your own iCloud with Apple.

Who is responsible

Fietsert (cycling app for iPhone and Apple Watch, coming soon to Android) and Streamert (internet radio for iPhone, Apple TV and Android) are apps by Joost Wellen, trading as AppertLabs. Joost Wellen is the controller under the GDPR. This privacy policy covers both apps and this website (appert.nu).

Contact: support@appert.nu

No tracking and no ads

Our apps show no advertising and contain no third-party analytics or tracking software. We don’t build a profile of you and we don’t sell or share data with advertisers. There is no automated decision-making or profiling.

Fietsert: data on your device

  • Location – to centre the map on your position, load nearby junctions and guide you. Only with your permission in your phone’s settings. On iPhone, the app uses your location in the background only while you are navigating a route.
  • Routes and settings – stored on your device. On iPhone and Apple Watch, if iCloud is on, they are synced through your own iCloud account with Apple (in your private iCloud database). We have no access to it. On Android they stay on your phone.
  • Apple Health (iPhone) – after a ride the app asks whether you want to save it as a cycling workout. Only if you choose to, the workout, distance, energy burned and the route you rode are saved in Apple Health. The app reads nothing from Health. This data does not come to us; it stays on your device and, if you have set this up in Health, in your own iCloud.
  • Apple Watch – the Watch app uses your location for the map and navigation, and while navigating it starts a cycling workout session so the app stays on screen; that session is discarded when you stop and is not saved in Health. Routes and the state of an ongoing navigation reach your Watch through your own iCloud.
  • Photos for a photo route – only the photo you choose or take. The junction numbers are read from the photo on your device (on iPhone with Apple’s text recognition). The photo is not sent anywhere.
  • Sharing a route – when you share a route, the app creates a route file that you send yourself through your phone’s share menu.
  • Usage counters – the app keeps a few simple counters on your device. They are not sent anywhere.

Fietsert: services the app uses

Fietsert has no server of its own. For maps, search, routes and weather the app connects directly to the services below. With every connection the service receives your IP address and technical details of the request (such as the time and the name of the app). These parties are responsible for what they do with that data themselves; their own privacy policy applies.

  • Apple Maps (iPhone and Apple Watch) – the map and, when you need to cycle to the junction network, the cycling route there. Apple receives the map area you view and, for directions, the start and end point (coordinates). See Apple’s privacy policy.
  • OpenStreetMap map tiles (Android) – the map on Android comes from the OpenStreetMap Foundation’s tile servers (tile.openstreetmap.org). They receive which map tiles you load, and so the area you view. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation’s privacy policy.
  • Nominatim (search, all platforms) – when you search for a place, address or junction, or choose your free area, your search text goes to the OpenStreetMap Foundation’s Nominatim (nominatim.openstreetmap.org). So please don’t type personal details there.
  • Overpass API (junction network, all platforms) – junctions, cycle routes and break spots (such as cafés) come from OpenStreetMap through public Overpass servers: overpass-api.de and lz4.overpass-api.de (FOSSGIS e.V., Germany), overpass.openstreetmap.fr (OpenStreetMap France) and overpass.kumi.systems (Kumi Systems). The app tries them in turn. For your free area the server receives the centre point (the coordinates of the place you choose, or your current location if you choose that) with a radius. For break spots during a ride the server receives your current position with a radius. To download a province the server receives only the province name. The Apple Watch also fetches junctions around your free area itself.
  • OSRM (routes to the network, Android only) – on Android the cycling route to the junction network comes from the public demo server of the OSRM open-source project (router.project-osrm.org). It receives the start and end point (coordinates).
  • Weather: Apple WeatherKit (iPhone and Apple Watch) – for the wind when planning a loop, the app sends your location (coordinates) to Apple WeatherKit, which returns the forecast. Apple’s privacy policy applies. On Android Fietsert shows no weather and uses no weather service.
  • iCloud (iPhone and Apple Watch) – syncing of routes, settings, your free area and your Plus provinces through your own iCloud account, only if iCloud is on.

Fietsert Plus: purchases

Fietsert Plus is a yearly subscription. You buy and pay for it through Apple’s App Store (iPhone) or Google Play (Android). Apple or Google handles payment, renewal and any refund, and is responsible for that itself. We receive no payment details (such as card or bank details). The app only sees whether Plus is active. Apple and Google send us sales and payout reports for our accounts.

You manage or cancel your subscription yourself: on iPhone in Settings > [your name] > Subscriptions, on Android in Google Play > Payments & subscriptions. See also Apple’s privacy policy and Google’s privacy policy.

Streamert

Streamert has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. To work, the app connects to the services below; they receive your IP address when it does:

  • Radio stations – the stream and the title of the current song come directly from the server of the station you pick.
  • Cover art – to find a cover, the app looks up the artist and title of the playing song in music databases such as iTunes (Apple), Deezer, MusicBrainz and Cover Art Archive. No account or login cookie is used for this.
  • Your own Wi-Fi network – to keep your iPhone and Apple TV in sync and to find and control Sonos speakers. This traffic stays on your local network.
  • Apple Music – only when you tap the Apple Music button yourself does the app open the song as a search in Apple Music.

Your settings and favourites are stored on your device. Streamert does not ask for access to your location, microphone, camera or contacts.

This website

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., US). GitHub may log visitors’ IP addresses on its own servers for security and operations; see GitHub’s privacy statement. The website itself uses no cookies, no analytics and no tracking, and loads no external fonts or scripts.

If you email us

If you email support@appert.nu, we use your email address, your name if you include it, and the content of your message only to answer your question.

Why we may do this (legal bases)

  • Using your location and search text and sending them to the map, search, routing and weather services above – necessary to provide the feature you ask for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). Your phone’s permission prompt for location, camera or photos is a technical switch that lets you decide what the app may do; it is not the legal basis.
  • Syncing through iCloud – necessary to provide the feature you turn on (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • Workouts in Apple Health – this is health data. It is saved only if you explicitly choose to after the ride (consent, Art. 6(1)(a) and Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw that consent at any time in the Health settings.
  • Fietsert Plus – necessary to perform your subscription (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). We keep the sales reports because tax law requires it (Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR).
  • Emails to support – our legitimate interest in answering your question (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), or performing your subscription if your question is about it (point b).
  • This website – our legitimate interest in keeping the site secure and available (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

How long data is kept

  • On your device – until you delete the routes or the app.
  • In iCloud – until you delete it, for example in Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Manage Storage.
  • In Apple Health – until you delete the workout in Health.
  • Emails to support – for no more than one year after your question has been dealt with.
  • Sales and payout reports – seven years, the Dutch tax retention period.
  • At the services above – according to their own privacy policies. The OpenStreetMap Foundation says it keeps server logs only temporarily, for operations and security.

Data outside the EU

Apple, Google and GitHub are international companies that may process data outside the European Economic Area, including in the United States. The OpenStreetMap Foundation is based in the United Kingdom and stores data in the United Kingdom and the Netherlands; its map tiles are delivered through a worldwide network of servers, so a request may also be handled outside the EEA. These parties are responsible for appropriate safeguards for such transfers themselves; see their privacy policies. We don’t transfer any data ourselves, because we receive no data from the apps.

Children

The apps ask for no account, name, age or email address and are not specifically aimed at children. We don’t knowingly collect data from children.

Your rights

You have the right to access, rectification (correction), erasure, restriction of processing, objection and portability of your data, and the right to withdraw any consent you gave. Because we receive no data from the apps, there is usually nothing for us to show or delete. You manage and delete data on your device, in iCloud or in Apple Health yourself in your phone’s settings. For data held by Apple, Google or the other services above, please contact those parties. For a request to us, email support@appert.nu; we reply within one month.

Complaints

If you are unhappy with how we handle your data, please tell us first. You always have the right to lodge a complaint with the Dutch Data Protection Authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.

Changes

If the apps start handling data differently, we update this policy and change the date at the top. We keep the Dutch and English versions the same.
